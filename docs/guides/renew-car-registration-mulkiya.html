How to renew your Mulkiya

Insurance first, fines cleared, a test if the car is over three years old. There is a 30 day grace period and then it gets expensive.

Check this first

Fees and procedures change without notice. Before you act on anything below, confirm it at the RTA, which is the authority that actually handles this.

The short version

  • Where: Your emirate's transport authority, or an approved testing centre.
  • Cost: AED 350 to 500, plus roughly AED 120 to 170 for the inspection if one is needed.
  • Takes: Same day. Minutes online if no inspection is required.
  • If you are late: Fines apply after the 30 day grace period. Driving unregistered risks around AED 500 and impound.
  • Valid for: 1 year, plus a 30 day grace period.

The steps

  1. Renew or confirm your car insurance first. It has to cover the new registration year, so doing this second wastes a trip.
  2. Pay any outstanding traffic fines. Renewal is blocked until they are cleared.
  3. If the car is more than three years old, it needs a technical inspection.
  4. Renew online through your emirate's portal, or in person at a testing centre.

The part people get wrong

The order. Insurance has to be in place before the registration can renew, and outstanding fines block the whole thing. People arrive at a testing centre, discover a fine from four months ago, and lose the afternoon.

The grace period is also not permission to drive. An unregistered car is a separate offence from a late renewal, and impound is on the table.
